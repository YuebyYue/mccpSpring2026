From Delivering Content to Designing Learning
A short reflection on how this course changed my view of teaching
Outcome focus
Constructive alignment
Meaningful interaction

4 slides | about 2.5 minutes | English reflection slice

In this short reflection, I want to explain one major change in my thinking about teaching. Before this course, I mostly judged teaching by whether content was clearly delivered and whether the task was completed. Now I see teaching more as the design of learning: starting from outcomes, aligning activities and assessment, and making interaction support real thinking.
2
My Main Shift: From Content Delivery to Learning Outcomes

REFLECTION OBTL This course introduced me to Outcomes-Based Teaching and Learning. It pushed me to ask not only what I teach, but what students can actually do by the end.

Before

  • I focused on whether the explanation was clear.
  • I cared about whether the procedure looked complete.
  • I often equated finishing the task with successful teaching.

Now

  • I start with the intended learning outcomes, or ILOs.
  • I ask whether the design supports understanding, not just delivery.
  • I see teaching as a process of enabling learning, not displaying content.
Key realization: Completing a teaching task does not automatically mean that learning has happened.
That shift also made me notice a weakness in my own design habits.
The biggest change for me came from OBTL. Before the course, I paid more attention to content delivery. I wanted the explanation to be smooth, the structure to be complete, and the task to be finished. After learning about OBTL, I began to focus on intended learning outcomes. The important question is no longer only what I present, but what students are able to understand, apply, or reflect on after the lesson.
3
What I Noticed: Execution Mindset and Misalignment

ALIGNMENT SELF-CRITIQUE In practice, I often worked from the content outward. I concentrated on progress and presentation, but reflected less on whether the design truly served the learning goal.

ILOs

Outcomes

What learners should know or be able to do.

TLAs

Activities

What learners do in order to reach those outcomes.

AMs

Assessment

How learning is checked and made visible.

My problem

I was usually planning from content presentation, not from the alignment among ILOs, TLAs, and AMs. That meant some classroom choices looked organized, but were not fully connected to the learning outcome.
A clear example of this was how I originally understood classroom interaction.
This new perspective also helped me recognize a problem in my own approach. I tend to be execution-driven. I think about how to move the task forward, how to meet the requirement, and how to present the content. But I do not always stop and ask whether my activities and assessment are aligned with the intended learning outcome. In other words, I can produce a well-organized task without achieving constructive alignment.
4
What I Will Change in Future Teaching

ACTION INTERACTION During our presentation design, I realized that a demo is not the same as a learning activity. A demo may simplify content, but students can still remain passive.

1. Start with ILOs

Define what students should achieve before planning content or materials.

2. Align the design

Choose activities and assessment methods that directly support the intended outcome.

3. Redesign interaction

Use question-driven tasks, discussion, or online tools so students make judgments rather than only watch.

Takeaway: The value of interaction is not that it makes class look active. It matters only when it genuinely pushes students to think and learn.
For me, the course changed teaching from a task of explaining content into a process of intentionally designing learning.
The final lesson for me concerns interaction. In our presentation design, we used a simplified demo because many classmates might not have a coding background. That choice lowered the barrier, but after reflecting on it, I realized that demo does not equal learning activity. Watching is still mostly passive. In future teaching, I want to begin with ILOs, align activities and assessment, and design interaction that requires students to judge, discuss, or decide. For me, this course changed teaching from explaining content to intentionally designing learning.
