## Slide 1. From Delivering Content to Designing Learning

In this short reflection, I want to explain one important change in how I understand teaching.  
Before taking this course, I tended to think about teaching in a content-oriented way. I focused on whether the explanation was clear, whether the process looked complete, and whether the task was successfully finished.  

However, this course introduced me to Outcomes-Based Teaching and Learning, or OBTL. That idea shifted my attention from what the teacher says to what students can actually do at the end of learning. So now, I no longer see teaching simply as delivering content. I see it more as designing conditions that support learning.

---

## Slide 2. My Main Shift: From Content Delivery to Learning Outcomes

The biggest change for me is that I now start from intended learning outcomes, or ILOs.  
In the past, I often judged teaching by presentation quality. If the content seemed organized and the explanation was smooth, I felt the teaching had worked. But now I realize that clear delivery alone is not enough.

What matters more is whether students have actually developed the understanding or ability we hoped for. This means teaching should not be centered only on content. Instead, it should be centered on learning results, and every teaching choice should support those results.

My key realization is this: completing a teaching task does not automatically mean that learning has happened.

---

## Slide 3. What I Noticed: Execution Mindset and Misalignment

This perspective also helped me notice a weakness in my own approach. I often have an execution mindset. I think first about how to move the task forward, how to meet the requirement, and how to present the material. But I do not always pause and ask whether my design is truly aligned with the learning goal.

That is where the idea of constructive alignment became important for me. I learned that effective teaching requires alignment among ILOs, TLAs, and AMs: intended learning outcomes, teaching and learning activities, and assessment methods.

I realized that I was often designing from content presentation outward, rather than from alignment inward. As a result, some activities looked well prepared, but they were not always strongly connected to the actual learning outcome.

---

## Slide 4. What I Will Change in Future Teaching

One clear example is how I used to understand interaction. In our presentation design, we used a simplified demo because many classmates might not have a coding background. That choice made the content easier to follow, but after reflecting on it, I realized that a demo is not the same as a learning activity.

Students can watch a demo quite passively. Real interaction should ask them to judge, discuss, choose, or respond. So in the future, I want to make three changes: first, start with ILOs; second, align activities and assessment with those outcomes; and third, design interaction that genuinely promotes thinking.

Overall, this course changed my understanding of teaching. For me, teaching is no longer mainly about explaining content. It is about intentionally designing learning.
